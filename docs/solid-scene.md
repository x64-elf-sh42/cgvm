# Solid Scene Demonstration

Status: implemented. Requires GPU revision 0.5.

This reference describes the earlier local test scene. The current default program
is [Solar System flight](solar-system.md).

## Bodies and scale

| Body | World center | Radius | Exclusion radius |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Terra (planet) | (-80,20,220) | 60 | 68 |
| Sol (star) | (220,100,550) | 100 | 108 |

These are local test objects, not a physically scaled Solar System. The ship and
asteroid sizes remain unchanged. No orbital motion, travel mode, landing, or
solar arcs are implemented.

The planet uses 80 triangles at center distances of 200 units or greater, and
168 triangles at smaller distances. The sun uses a fixed 120-triangle mesh.
Selection is performed in guest code using squared distance from vehicle position
to planet center. There is no hysteresis or crossfade; a silhouette change can be
visible at the threshold. The exclusion sphere conservatively encloses either mesh.

Geometry, material colors, outward face normals, and directions to the fixed sun
are generated by scripts/generate_solids.py and loaded as guest constants. Planet
materials are blue water, green patches, and pale polar faces. The sun uses varied
yellow/orange materials without lighting attenuation.

## Guest rendering pipeline

Guest instructions select geometry, reject back faces using the outward face
normal and camera position, transform vertices to camera space, clip polygons,
compute perspective coordinates and reciprocal depth, and issue screen-space
triangles. Planet intensity is evaluated by guest code as
0.32 + 0.68*max(0, dot(normal, light_direction)); each material channel is multiplied
by intensity and truncated. Light directions are precomputed because both bodies
are stationary. There are no textures or host-side 3D transforms.

Near-plane clipping uses Sutherland–Hodgman clipping against camera_z >= 0.5.
For each directed triangle edge, an inside-to-outside transition emits its
intersection; an outside-to-inside transition emits its intersection followed by
the inside endpoint. An inside-to-inside transition emits the endpoint, and an
outside-to-outside transition emits nothing. Intersections use linear camera-space
interpolation. Vertices exactly on the plane count as inside. The resulting
polygon contains at most four vertices and is triangulated as a fan. Degenerate
triangles are harmless GPU no-ops. Perspective division occurs only after clipping.

Guest scratch at 9000 stores three transformed vertices; scratch at 9100 stores
up to four clipped vertices. R12 is the output pointer and R13 the vertex count.
These are guest-program conventions, not architectural device registers.

Triangles and asteroid/ship lines share the working depth buffer. The HUD disables
depth. Frame clear resets depth, so previous geometry cannot obstruct a later
frame. Sphere triangles are not distance-culled in this small scene.

## Surface exclusion

Guest code computes a candidate position and compares its squared distance to
each body's exclusion radius squared. A candidate inside a boundary is projected
radially back to radius + 0.001 unit. The correction removes inward penetration
while retaining the candidate's tangential component, allowing an angled approach
to slide around the sphere. A direct radial approach has essentially zero forward
displacement. The throttle setting is preserved; steering away restores free
movement without needing to accelerate again. The speed gauge therefore shows
commanded speed, not ground-relative motion while the limiter is active.

Distance and normalization are computed by guest Newton iterations (32 iterations
for this local scene); no host collision helper is involved. Contact sets R14 to
30. It counts down once per simulation update and is refreshed by subsequent
contact. The HUD displays SURFACE LIMIT while this counter is nonzero.

This is an endpoint correction, not a swept collision solver. At the current
maximum speed a step is at most 1/6 unit. The eight-unit margin protects the surface
and six-unit chase-camera displacement. Faster travel needs swept collision.
The program assumes an initially valid exterior position; arbitrary injected
positions at a body's exact center are not a supported recovery case. Asteroids
remain non-colliding. Camera behavior and exclusion radii are unchanged.

## Proximity display

The guest computes minimum clearance to the planet and sun exclusion spheres and
stores it as binary32 at address 8400. Clearance means distance to the safety
boundary, not altitude above the visible mesh. The lower-left CLEARANCE readout
shows whole world units, clamped to [0,999]. The adjacent gauge spans 0–20 units;
its right endpoint means 20 or more. Within 20 units the display turns amber and
PROXIMITY appears above the scene. SURFACE LIMIT overrides that warning during
contact and for 30 subsequent updates (half a second at 60 Hz).

The labels, digits, marker, and warnings are rendered by guest line commands
with depth disabled. There is no host UI overlay or new device instruction.

## Validation

Tests compare all eight triangle inside/outside classifications and on-plane
geometry against an independent clipping reference. Flight tests cover near-plane
crossings, deterministic output, and the existing control behavior. A full-speed
approach verifies holding outside the planet, sustained throttle, tangential sliding, and escape
when facing outward. The approach remains on the 60 Hz guest publication schedule.
GPU tests cover overlapping triangles, line occlusion, depth reset, and extreme
screen-coordinate clipping. ASan/UBSan cover the approach test path.

The clearance and contact indicators include body names. A separate forward-ray
readout reports the named body ahead; see [Named navigation and steering](flight.md#named-navigation-and-steering).
